23 October 2026 Why this page exists

I keep thinking about things and then losing them. A conversation ends, a good idea goes into a chat window somewhere, and six months later it's gone. Writing things down in public — or semi-public — forces a different kind of attention. You can't hand-wave at something you have to actually write.

So this is the attempt. Not a blog with a publishing schedule, not a portfolio. Just a place where thoughts get parked once they're sticky enough to survive a day.

The rules I'm giving myself:

  • Short is fine. A paragraph counts.
  • Unfinished is fine. Mark it unfinished.
  • Being wrong in public is fine. Being vague is not.
The test for whether an idea is real: can I explain it to someone who disagrees without getting defensive?
Example entry On owning your tools

Every tool you depend on is a small bet that someone else keeps caring about it. Usually that's fine — that's how software works. But the bet gets expensive when the thing you've built on top becomes the thing you can't leave.

I've started asking a boring question about tools: what happens to my work if this disappears tomorrow? If the answer is "I lose everything", the tool is doing more than I agreed to.

Example entry A note on notes

Most note-taking systems fail at retrieval, not capture. Capture is solved — anything can capture. The problem is that a year later the notes exist but the context that made them meaningful is gone.

What seems to work instead: write the note as if to a stranger, and date it. Both cost almost nothing at write time and save an enormous amount at read time.
