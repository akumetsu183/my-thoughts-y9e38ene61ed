Why this page exists

I keep thinking about things and then losing them. A conversation ends, a good idea goes into a chat window somewhere, and six months later it's gone. Writing things down in public — or semi-public — forces a different kind of attention.

So this is the attempt. Not a blog with a publishing schedule, not a portfolio. Just a place where thoughts get parked once they're sticky enough to survive a day.

On owning your tools

Every tool you depend on is a small bet that someone else keeps caring about it. The bet gets expensive when the thing you've built on top becomes the thing you can't leave.

The question worth asking: what happens to my work if this disappears tomorrow?

Note · example
A note on notes

Most note-taking systems fail at retrieval, not capture. Capture is solved — anything can capture. A year later the notes exist but the context that made them meaningful is gone.

Write the note as if to a stranger, and date it. Both cost almost nothing at write time and save an enormous amount at read time.
