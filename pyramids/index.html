01The inputs

Every default is the mainstream or experimentally-measured value. Nothing is exaggerated to make the point — the point is that the honest numbers produce it.

—
The mean block at Giza is usually quoted as ~1 m³ and ~2.5 tonnes. Sliders use a cube of this edge length.
—
Depth cut per hour, per side, by a two-person sawing team. Calibrated to Denys Stocks' 1999 Aswan experiments: a 1.8 m copper blade with sand cut a 75–95 cm long slot to 3 cm deep in 14 hours (≈2.1 mm/hr). Granite was the test stone — limestone cuts faster, which is what the upper end of this slider allows.
—
20 years is the mainstream figure, matching Khufu's reign (23 years on the Turin king list). Herodotus says 20. The Wadi al-Jarf papyri put work running to the end of the reign.
—
Daylight-bound labour: 12 h is the maximum anyone quotes. 9 h is a generous real-world average with sand replenishment, tool re-annealing and rest. Copper work-hardens and has to be re-annealed constantly.
—
The flood season (Akhet) swallowed months of the year. 120 days = inundation-only labour; 300–360 = year-round corvée. Mainstream models need year-round work, so the default is generous.
—
Everyone who doesn't cut stone: sand carriers, water, tool re-annealing, food preparation, bakers, brewers, housing, laundry, medical, scribes, overseers. A ratio of 1.0 means one support person per cutter — conservative against most reconstructions.

04So what?

—

The arithmetic above does not prove anything about who built the pyramid. It shows something narrower and more useful: the mainstream numbers, taken at face value and combined with the experimentally measured cutting rates, do not close. Something in the model is wrong — the cutting rate, the timeline, the block count, or the labour assumption. Those are the only options.

05Where the numbers come from

  • Denys A. Stocks, Experiments in Egyptian Archaeology: Stoneworking Technology in Ancient Egypt (Routledge, 2003) and "Stone sarcophagus manufacture in ancient Egypt", Antiquity 73 (1999) — the Aswan copper saw/drill trials: 1.8 m blade, 15 cm deep, 6 mm thick, 14.5 kg; 3 cm deep slot in 14 hours; blade lost 7.5 mm of depth; copper tube + dry quartz sand drilled granite at 5.2 cm³/hr with a three-person crew.
  • Mark Lehner / Zahi Hawass — 20,000–30,000 peak labour force; NOVA and AERA excavation reports on the workers' city and cemetery at Giza.
  • IEEE Spectrum, "How Many People Did It Take to Build the Great Pyramid?" (June 2020) — the physics-based floor of ~3,300 construction workers, doubling to under 7,000 with support; 2.6 million m³ volume, ~6.75 Mt mass.
  • Scheuring, npj Heritage Science 13:473 (2025) — 2.3 million blocks, mean block ~2.5 t, ~16-hour workdays, pace of one block per 1–3 minutes; ~3,000 m³ of Aswan granite (~0.1% of volume) up to 60 t.
  • Wadi al-Jarf papyri — the logbook of the inspector Merer's boat crew, ferrying Tura limestone; documents year 27 of Khufu's reign.
